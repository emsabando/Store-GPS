# RMS Titanic (1912) – General Arrangement of the Lower Decks
## D (Saloon), E (Upper), F (Middle), G (Lower), Orlop, Lower Orlop and Tank Top

Research note for the free-roam 3D recreation. Compiled 2026-11-04 from primary and near-primary web sources. Every number carries its unit and a metric conversion (1 ft = 0.3048 m); every fact carries a URL. Where sources disagree both values are shown and a preferred value is chosen with reasoning.

**Conventions used throughout**

* **Distance from bow** is measured from the **forward perpendicular (FP)** at the load waterline. The British Wreck Commissioner's compartment lengths sum to exactly 850 ft (259.1 m), i.e. the length between perpendiculars, so the 15 bulkhead positions below are FP-referenced. Titanic's stem is nearly plumb, so the stem-head at forecastle level is only a few feet forward of the FP; almost all of the 32 ft 9 in (10.0 m) difference between LOA (882 ft 9 in / 269.1 m) and LBP is the counter-stern overhang. Source for LOA/beam/draught: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic ; compartment lengths: https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepWC.php
* **Frames**: numbered outward from the (unnumbered) midship frame at 425 ft (129.5 m) from the FP, suffixed F (forward) or A (aft); frame spacing 3 ft (0.914 m) amidships, reduced to 24 in (0.61 m) forward and 27 in (0.686 m) aft (https://hercolano2.blogspot.com/2012/04/titanic-double-bottom.html – Halpern, "Titanic's Hidden Deck"; https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/building-of-the-hulls.html). Secondary sources give the forward-most frame as 156F at the FP and the aftmost as 148A at the sternpost (https://titanicconnections.com/tag/titanic-construction-images/). In the 3-ft midbody, *distance from FP ≈ 425 − 3·N* for frame N·F and *425 + 3·N* for frame N·A. Check: Encyclopedia Titanica places bulkhead D at frame 78F (https://encyclopedia-titanica.org/an-analysis-of-titanics-vertical-and-lateral-watertight-doors-20167.html) → 425 − 234 = 191 ft vs. 193 ft from the inquiry sum: consistent.
* **Heights** are above the top of the keel at the centreline unless stated. Load draught 34 ft 7 in (10.54 m) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic).
* **Port/starboard** are given as seen facing forward. Full beam = 92 ft 6 in (28.19 m).

* Summary of heights: bulkheads A and B and K–P rise to D deck; C–J rise only to E deck (https://www.titanicology.com/FloodingByCompartment.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Titanic). Mark Chirnside quotes the as-built description "the majority ... extended to the saloon deck, D, and the remainder to the upper deck, E" (https://markchirnside.co.uk/tag/watertight-bulkheads/).
* The 12 doors "immediately above the inner bottom" in the engine and boiler spaces were Harland & Wolff vertical, gravity-closing, cast-iron doors released by an electro-magnet from the bridge, closing in 25–30 s, also closable by a hand lever and by float (https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepStructure.php). Wikipedia counts 11 vertical doors on the orlop/tank-top level (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic) — the twelfth is the extra door at the after end of the bunker passage behind bulkhead D.
* For bulkheads E, F, G, H and J the tank-top door was not on the bulkhead but at the end of a watertight passage about 9 ft (2.7 m) long leading through the transverse coal bunker into the boiler room (botRepWC).
* Doors on the Orlop–G, F–E and E–D levels were horizontal sliding doors operated by a key, e.g. along Scotland Road (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic).
* Design standard: afloat with any two adjoining compartments flooded with 2 ft 6 in – 3 ft (0.76–0.91 m) freeboard to the bulkhead deck; Wilding: would also float with any three of the forward compartments flooded (https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepStructure.php ; https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTInq20Wilding01.php). 73 watertight compartments in all, 29 of them above the inner bottom.
* Other watertight flats: G deck in the after peak; the Orlop deck over the shaft tunnels (between the tunnel bulkheads); the Orlop deck in the forepeak tank (botRepStructure).
* The electric engine room is flanked by six watertight fresh-water tanks (792 tons total: 155, 149 and 92 tons per side) set in from the shell (Halpern, hercolano2 link above).

## 3. D Deck (Saloon Deck)

Continuous bow to stern. Height to C deck 10 ft 6 in (3.20 m) amidships, 9 ft (2.74 m) forward, 9 ft 6 in (2.90 m) aft (https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepAccomo.php). Height above keel ≈ 54 ft 7 in (16.6 m) amidships (derived, §1.2).

### 8.2 Boilers, bunkers, stokeholds, ash ejectors, funnels

* **29 boilers: 24 double-ended + 5 single-ended**; all 15 ft 9 in (4.80 m) diameter; double-ended 20 ft (6.10 m) long with 6 furnaces; single-ended 11 ft 9 in (3.58 m) long with 3 furnaces; 159 furnaces; working pressure 215 psi (14.8 bar), tested 430 psi; heating surface 144,142 sq ft; grate area 3,466 sq ft; each boiler ≈ 91.5 t and holds 48.5 t of water; boilers in built steel cradles stayed to the ship's side and each other (https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepMach.php ; https://www.titanicology.com/Titanica/TitanicsPrimeMover.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic ; botRepGeneral).
* **Arrangement**: five boilers abreast per room (four in BR 6), set fore-and-aft, fired from stokeholds at each end of the double-ended boilers; **stokeholds numbered 1–11** (titanicology). Natural draught assisted by fans blowing into the open stokeholds (botRepMach); 5 stokehold indicators per stokehold (ggarchives 04). Per 4-hour watch: 48 firemen, 20 trimmers, 5 leading firemen (titanicology).
* **Coal bunkers**: transverse bunkers at the watertight bulkheads between boiler rooms (with the 9 ft watertight passages), 'tween-deck bunkers on G deck in way of the boiler rooms, plus the No. 3 hold reserve bunker; capacity 6,611 tonnes plus 1,092 t in Hold 3; ≈ 600–650 t burned per day (≈ 8 days at full speed); coaling ports above F deck; ≈ 100 t of ash per day (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic ; titanicology).
* **Ash ejectors**: one in each large boiler compartment (also usable as circulating/feed pumps), except three boiler rooms that used ballast-and-bilge pumps (botRepMach).
* **Funnels and uptakes** (§9): four elliptical funnels 24 ft 6 in × 19 ft (7.47 × 5.79 m) in section, average ≈ 150 ft (45.7 m) above the furnace bars / ≈ 70 ft (21.3 m) above the casing; the three forward funnels take the boiler gases, "the uptakes ... were united immediately above the watertight bulkhead which separated the boiler rooms", i.e. **funnel 1 = BR 6 + BR 5 (over bulkhead E, ≈ 247 ft / 75 m)**, **funnel 2 = BR 4 + BR 3 (over bulkhead G, ≈ 361 ft / 110 m)**, **funnel 3 = BR 2 + BR 1 (over bulkhead J, ≈ 478 ft / 146 m)**; **funnel 4 stands over the turbine hatch as a ventilator (≈ 595 ft / 181 m) and carries the galley flues**, plus exhausts from the smoking room/restaurant per secondary sources (https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepGeneral.php ; ggarchives 04; titanicology; https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/community/threads/fourth-funnel-a-vent.17707/page-2). Steam whistles on the two forward funnels (botRepWC).
* **Pumps**: 5 ballast/bilge pumps at 250 t/h, 3 bilge pumps at 150 t/h, 10 in ballast main fore and aft, double bilge main forward of BR 5 and aft of BR 1 (botRepMach); total pumping capacity ≈ 1,700 long tons/hour (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_the_Titanic).

### 8.3 Main engines

* **Reciprocating engines (2)**: four-crank inverted direct-acting triple-expansion; cylinders HP 54 in (1.372 m), IP 84 in (2.134 m), two LP 97 in (2.464 m); stroke 6 ft 3 in / 75 in (1.905 m); order from forward LP–HP–IP–LP; 15,000 ihp each at 75 rpm design, 78 rpm normal full speed; crank angles 106°/100°/54°/100°; Brown's steam-hydraulic reversing engine; bedplate 195 tons, each column 21 tons, heaviest cylinder with liner 50 tons; **height ≈ 30 ft (9.1 m)**, weight ≈ 1,000 tons each (titanicology) – Wikipedia gives 720 t + 195 t bedplate and "63 ft (19 m) long" (the latter is doubtful: the whole engine room is 69 ft). Preferred: 30 ft tall, ≈ 720–1,000 t depending on what is counted. Wing propellers 3-bladed, 23.5 ft (7.2 m) diameter. (https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepMach.php ; https://www.titanicology.com/Titanica/TitanicsPrimeMover.htm ; ggarchives 04; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic)
* **Turbine (1)**: Parsons low-pressure reaction, direct-coupled, ahead only; rotor 12 ft (3.66 m) diameter, 12 ft 8 in (3.86 m) long; blades 18 in → 25½ in; ≈ 16,000 shp at 165 rpm (175–180 rpm at 80 rpm on the reciprocators, max 190); weight 420 tons; engages above 50 rpm; centre propeller 17 ft (5.2 m) diameter (16.5 ft design). Total plant 46,000 hp normal, ≈ 59,000 hp at 83 rpm (titanicology; Wikipedia).
* **Shafting**: triple screw; 14 thrust collars per wing shaft; thrust blocks in the turbine room (titanicology).

### 8.4 Refrigeration machinery

Two horizontal duplex CO₂ machines by J. & E. Hall, Dartford (four independent units), on the **port side of the reciprocating engine room at starting-platform level**, each with its own steam condenser and gunmetal circulating pump; ice-melting capacity 84 tons; four brine evaporators in an insulated room at Orlop level directly above, three vertical duplex brine pumps, brine return tanks above those; two ice-making tanks; cold rooms aft on G and Orlop (§7), insulated cargo chamber aft, cold larders and bottle coolers in pantries throughout (https://www.ggarchives.com/OT/Titanic/25-ImageLibrary/TheShipbuilder-05-Refrigeration.html).

### 8.5 Firemen's passage (tunnel) and spiral staircases

* Two vertical **spiral staircases**, one each side of the centreline immediately abaft the firemen's quarters, descend from D deck (through E, F, G and Orlop levels) to the forward end of a **tunnel immediately above the tank top** running aft to the forward stokehold (BR 6), so firemen never crossed passenger spaces (botRepAccomo). Shipbuilder: "two spiral staircases and a tunnel through the forward hold" (ggarchives 08).
* The passage is watertight throughout, carries the forward pumping pipes and valves and the windlass steam/exhaust pipes, and is closed at its after end by a vertical sliding watertight door of the tank-top pattern at **bulkhead D (frame 78F, ≈ 193 ft / 59 m from the FP)** (botRepAccomo; ET door analysis). Derived length ≈ 100 ft (≈ 31 m) from the stairs near bulkhead B (≈ 91 ft) through holds 2 and 3 to bulkhead D; drawn on the centreline of the tank top on Plate V.
* Flooding: water overtopping G deck forward "tumbled down and flooded the circular ladders and tunnel" (https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/community/threads/firemans-passageway-wtds-and-other-miscellany.35688).

## 11. Access and circulation summary (useful for pathing)

* Forward Grand Staircase: Boat deck → E deck (7 levels), over 60 ft (18 m) high, 16 ft (4.9 m) wide, with 3 elevators E→A on its fore side; a single flight continues E → F (baths). Between funnels 1 and 2, ≈ 280–309 ft from FP (derived). (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Staircase_of_the_Titanic ; botRepAccomo)
* Aft Grand Staircase: A–C decks only, between funnels 3 and 4 (same).
* Stewards' staircase between the reciprocating-engine casing and the funnel-3 casing: Boat deck down to E (botRepAccomo).
* Forward 2nd-class stair + elevator: Boat deck → F deck (M–N, ≈ 645 ft). Aft 2nd-class stair: B → G (N–O). (botRepAccomo)
* Aft 3rd-class main entrance under the poop: C → G, 16 ft wide to E, 8 ft E–F (botRepAccomo). Forward 3rd-class: four ladderways from the D-deck open space; two wide stairs from the forward well deck.
* Side (gangway) doors: D deck – 1 baggage door starboard in the 3rd-class open space, 2 each side at the forward 1st-class entrance; E deck – 1 each side at the forward end of Scotland Road, 1 port abreast the engine room, 1 each side at the forward 2nd-class entrance; the teak accommodation ladder works from either E-deck 2nd-class gangway (botRepStructure).
* Boiler-room escapes: fidley ladders in each boiler casing to the Boat deck and into Scotland Road (botRepAccomo).

Passenger capacity (maximum, higher alternative class counted): 1,034 first, 510 second, 1,022 third = 2,566; crew ≈ 75 deck, 326 engine, 544 victualling (botRepAccomo).
