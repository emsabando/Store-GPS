# RMS Titanic (1912) — Principal dimensions, hull form and exterior fittings

Research dossier for the free-roam 3D recreation. Every number carries its unit, a metric conversion (1 ft = 0.3048 m; 1 in = 25.4 mm; 1 long ton = 1.016 t) and a source URL. Where sources disagree both values are recorded and a **recommended value** is given with reasoning.

**Coordinate convention used below.** "ft aft of FP" = distance aft of the *forward perpendicular* (the stem at the load waterline). The stem overhang above the waterline is ≈1 ft, so "ft from bow" ≈ "ft aft of FP" + 1 ft. The aft perpendicular (rudder post) is at 850 ft (259.08 m) aft of the FP; the extreme stern (counter) is at ≈883 ft (269.1 m). Port = left looking forward, starboard = right. Heights are above the **keel base** (top of flat keel) unless stated.

**Measurement note.** Several longitudinal positions were not available as published numbers. They were measured by this research from the public-domain *Engineering* side elevation of 26 May 1911 (Wikimedia Commons, "Titanic side plan annotated English.png", 3,735 × 1,180 px, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Titanic_side_plan_annotated_English.png). The drawing was calibrated by least-squares fit of the fifteen annotated watertight bulkheads (A–P) against the compartment lengths in the British Wreck Commissioner's Report (sum = 850 ft = length between perpendiculars). Fit: 3.971 px/ft, residuals ≤ 1.7 ft on all 15 bulkheads; the fitted stem and counter reproduce the official 882 ft 9 in overall length to 0.2 %. Vertical check: keel→waterline measured 34.5 ft (official 34 ft 7 in), keel→boat deck 92.2 ft (official 92 ft 6 in). Values flagged **[measured]** carry ±2 ft (±0.6 m) uncertainty. Lifeboat stations were taken from the Commons boat-deck plan (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Titanic_Boat_Deck_plan_with_lifeboats.png) mapped onto the elevation; ±5 ft.

Sources blocked to automated access (Encyclopedia Titanica, Titanic Connections) are cited through their search snippets; those facts are marked **[snippet]** and should be re-checked by a human if load-bearing.

### 3.1 Navigating bridge
- Open bridge with roof, 8 ft (2.4 m) high; extends to both ship's sides into enclosed **wing cabs** (windows on three sides, overhanging the hull); ET quotes a width of 27 ft for the central bridge structure [snippet] https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/community/threads/16989/ ; Wikipedia.
- Wheelhouse at the aft end of the bridge: ≈20 ft wide (port–starboard) × 9 ft 6 in (fore–aft) (Bob Read estimate) [snippet]; five windows with blinds; main wheel 3 ft 6 in on a 32 in pedestal; chart room abaft.
- Bridge equipment: five order telegraphs (two engine, two docking, one emergency), telemotor wheel, standard and steering compasses, loud-speaking telephones to forecastle, crow's nest, engine room and docking bridge. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_navigation_bridge ; GG Archives Shipbuilder-08.
- Bridge rises ≈23 m above the waterline (Wikipedia) — consistent with 104 ft keel-to-bridge-top.

## 9. Name and port-of-registry lettering

- Name "TITANIC" on both bows (18 in / 457 mm high letters) and on the stern counter (18 in), with "LIVERPOOL" beneath on the stern in 12 in (305 mm) letters. Roman capitals, outlines incised into the shell by centre-punching/caulking, then painted (yellow/gold, "mustard", not gold leaf). Bow letters tilted to follow the sheer; stern letters follow the counter's curve. Sources [snippet]: https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/the-application-of-titanics-name-to-her-hull.html ; https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/community/threads/titanics-bow-name-plates.17471/ ; TRMA paint (same yellow as the stripe).
- Bow name position: on the forecastle shell plating a few feet below the forecastle deck edge, starting ≈15–20 ft abaft the stem (photo-based estimate).

## 12. Ventilators, skylights, deck furniture

- Mechanical ventilation (64 "Sirocco" fans by Davidson & Co.) meant the ship carried comparatively few traditional cowl ventilators; weather-deck cowls (white, with screened mouths) appear on the forecastle, the poop, the tank-room/engine casings and the boat-deck casings; large fan housings labelled "FAN" flank each boiler-uptake casing on the boat deck. Sources: https://www.trmaarchive.com/oly_vhl.shtml ; ET ventilator articles [snippet] https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/screens-over-intake-and-exhaust-ducts-of-titanics-ventilators.html ; Commons boat-deck plan.
- Bob Read's analysis concludes Titanic probably did **not** carry the two African-cowl ventilators on the officers' quarters roof seen on Olympic/Britannic (no wreck evidence): https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/african-cowl-ventilators-on-the-roof-of-titanics-officers-quarters.html [snippet].
- Skylights: raised roofs with skylights over the 1st-class lounge (with the compass platform), the 1st-class smoking room and the engine rooms (reciprocating-engine casing, turbine casing); E-deck skylight forward of E200/E201; Marconi and officers' lavatory skylights finished as teak, other skylights white. Sources: TRMA paint ; Commons boat-deck plan ; ET [snippet].
- Deck benches: teak slat benches with cast-iron ends along the deckhouses and rails on the boat deck/A deck (teak finish per TRMA); none alongside the aft 1st-class stairway skylight (Marschall letter https://trmaarchive.com/ken_letter.html).
- Deck chairs: folding wooden steamer chairs with cane seats (surviving example ≈37¾ × 22½ × 54 in); a reported count of ≈650 is unverified; A-deck plan marks "DECK CHAIRS" storage against bulkheads. Sources: https://www.shipsofscale.com/sosforums/goto/post?id=294262 [snippet] ; ET A-deck plan [snippet].
- Boat deck fittings on the Commons plan (forward to aft): wheelhouse & bridge, chart room, captain's and officers' cabins, Marconi room, funnel 1 casing with fan rooms, 1st-class entrance/grand staircase dome, gymnasium (starboard), funnel 2 casing, raised roof over the lounge with compass platform, funnel 3 casing, officers' mess, engineers' promenade with electric boat winches, tank room/dome over 2nd-class entrance, reciprocating-engine-room casing, funnel 4 within the raised roof over the smoking room, 2nd-class promenade, 2nd-class entrance, elevator. Steel bulwarks run along the boat deck edge between the forward and aft boat groups ("STEEL BULWARK P. & S.").

## 14. Gaps / items to verify by hand
1. Encyclopedia Titanica and Titanic Connections could only be read through search snippets (Cloudflare block): funnel heights, porthole sizes, anchor article, name lettering, colour guide — open in a browser to confirm.
2. Funnel height: 163 ft 8 in / 165 ft 2 in (modern) vs 175 ft (1911–12 publicity, 1911 elevation). Recommended modern values.
3. Mast rake (≈5–6° from the elevation) and exact mast base positions should be confirmed against the H&W rigging plan (NMNI HOYFM.2010.73.53).
4. Wing-propeller transverse offset and shaft height not in any text source consulted; use a body plan (NMNI lines plan HOYFM.2010.73.12).
5. Deck heights for F, G and orlop decks are estimates (8–8.5 ft tween-decks); E deck ≈11 ft above the waterline is from a forum post.
6. Porthole counts per row, rivet pitch and the sheer-stripe width are estimates pending the Beveridge GA plans.
7. Bulwark colours on forecastle/poop (black vs white) should be checked against photographs.
