# RMS Titanic (1912) — General arrangement of the upper decks: Boat Deck, A (Promenade), B (Bridge), C (Shelter)

Research report for the game geometry team. Every number carries its unit, feet first and metres in brackets (1 ft = 0.3048 m). Every fact carries a source URL. Where sources disagree, both values are given and the better-supported value is marked **(adopt)** with reasoning.

Conventions used throughout:

* **Distance from bow** = distance aft of the extreme forward point of the stem at the forecastle deck, in feet (metres). Positive aft.
* **Port** = left when facing the bow; **starboard** = right. Odd cabin/lifeboat numbers are starboard, even numbers port ([Wikipedia – Lifeboats of the Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeboats_of_the_Titanic); forum consensus on cabins B-51/53/55 starboard, B-52/54/56 port — see §7).
* "Measured" positions were taken by this researcher from the 1911 *Shipbuilder* general-arrangement plates (Olympic & Titanic, Midsummer 1911) hosted at GG Archives, calibrated against the hull length (882 ft 9 in) on the B- and C-deck plates and cross-checked against the 1912 *International Marine Engineering* Titanic profile. The 1911 plates were drawn for *Olympic*; Titanic differences (A-deck screen, B-deck suites/Café Parisien, officers' house) are noted. **Measurement uncertainty is ±10 ft (±3 m)** unless a written dimension is quoted.

### 1.2 Deck names

Boat Deck; A = Promenade Deck; B = Bridge Deck; C = Shelter Deck; D = Saloon Deck; E = Upper Deck; F = Middle Deck; G = Lower Deck; Orlop; Tank Top. Ten decks "excluding the top of the officers' quarters", eight used by passengers. Source: [Wikipedia – Titanic, Dimensions and layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic); plate titles in [GG Archives – Shipbuilder special plates](https://www.ggarchives.com/OT/Titanic/25-ImageLibrary/TheShipbuilder-10-Design-SpecialPlates.html).

## 3. Boat Deck

### 3.1 Extent and character

* Uncovered topmost deck, ≈500 ft (152 m) long, from the navigating bridge (190 ft / 57.9 m from the bow) to the second-class entrance house aft (≈684–690 ft / 208–210 m from bow). Lowest point 92 ft 6 in (28.19 m) above keel. Deck plated steel with wood planking (eight steel decks amidships). Sources: [BOT Report – Decks](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepAccomo.php); [Wilding](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTInq18Wilding01.php).
* Divided into four segregated promenades: **Officers' Promenade** (forward), **First Class Promenade** (midships), **Engineers' Promenade**, **Second Class Promenade** (aft) ([Wikipedia – Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic); labelled on [Shipbuilder Plate 3(a)](https://www.ggarchives.com/CVimages/OT/Titanic/TheShipbuilder/1911-Midsummer/Plate3a-BoatDeckPlan.jpg)).
* Lifeboats lined both sides except in the First Class area, where there was a deliberate gap ([Wikipedia – Olympic-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-class_ocean_liner)).
* First-class promenade space on the Boat Deck: "a space 200 ft long, extending the entire width of the ship" ([Shipbuilder 1911 via GG Archives](https://www.ggarchives.com/OT/Titanic/25-ImageLibrary/TheShipbuilder-06-PassengerAccommodation.html)); Wikipedia: "open-air promenade 200 ft long", "only four of the 20 lifeboats were on this part of the deck" ([Wikipedia – First-class facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_facilities_of_the_Titanic)).
* Second-class promenade: "a 145-foot-long space at the end of the boat deck" (Shipbuilder); "a 145 ft long unsheltered stretch … wrapping the raised roof of the first-class smoking room"; wooden-slatted wrought-iron benches; deck chairs hired at 3 s / $1 per voyage ([Wikipedia – Second- and third-class facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-_and_third-class_facilities_on_the_Titanic)).
* Stairways from the Boat Deck had a minimum effective width of 8 ft (2.44 m); the three first-class lifts ran E→A only, not to the Boat Deck; a second-class stairway and lift ran down to F deck; two vertical iron crew ladders led down to A deck; Wilding: ladders "go down two decks to B deck" ([BOT Report](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepAccomo.php); [Wilding Q.19863](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTInq18Wilding01.php)).

### 3.4 Lifeboat stations

## 4. A Deck — Promenade Deck

### 4.1 Extent

* Entirely first class. Promenade "over 500 ft long on each side of the deck and, in parts, exceeds 30 ft in width" ([Shipbuilder](https://www.ggarchives.com/OT/Titanic/25-ImageLibrary/TheShipbuilder-06-PassengerAccommodation.html)); superstructure 546 ft (166 m), ≈ 177–723 ft (54–220 m) from bow ([Wikipedia – Olympic-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-class_ocean_liner)). Deck house "extending nearly the whole length of the deck, varying in width from 24 ft to 72 ft" (7.3–21.9 m) ([BOT Report](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepAccomo.php)). Deck 83 ft 0 in (25.30 m) above keel (§1.3). Outer bulwarks capped with a teak rail; open at the sides except the forward enclosed section ([Wikipedia – First-class facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_facilities_of_the_Titanic)).
* **Forward enclosed promenade (Titanic only):** "the forward section was protected against the weather by a steel screen, 192 ft long, with large windows in it" ([BOT Report](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepAccomo.php)). The registration certificate (March 1912) excludes an open space "198 feet long, abreast windows port side" and the same to starboard ([Chirnside](https://markchirnside.co.uk/faq-did-the-enclosure-of-titanics-a-deck-promenade-increase-her-gross-tonnage/)); Encyclopedia Titanica: "half enclosed for a distance of 198 ft" ([ET A-deck page](https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/deckplan/A+Deck/)). The windows slid open ("steel screen with sliding windows", a late design change) ([Wikipedia – Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic); [Wikipedia – Olympic-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-class_ocean_liner)). **Adopt: screen from the forward end of A deck (≈177 ft) aft to ≈ 369–375 ft (112–114 m) from the bow, i.e. abreast funnel 2 and the forward end of the Reading & Writing Room.** Olympic's A-deck promenade was never enclosed ([Chirnside](https://markchirnside.co.uk/faq-did-the-enclosure-of-titanics-a-deck-promenade-increase-her-gross-tonnage/)). Chirnside's article "The Enclosure of Titanic's Forward A-deck Promenade: Popular Myth?" argues it compensated for the loss of B-deck promenade, not spray complaints ([ET article page](https://www.encyclopedia-titanica.org/the-enclosure-of-titanics-forward-a-deck-promenade-popular-myth.html)).
* The forward staircases from the bridge connect to either side of the A-deck promenade ([Wikipedia – navigation bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_navigation_bridge)).

## 5. B Deck — Bridge Deck

### 5.1 Extent

* The uppermost deck of the hull proper and the top strength deck; continuous for 550 ft (167.6 m) ≈ 175–725 ft (53–221 m) from bow, with wells ≈ 50 ft (15 m) fore and aft; forecastle deck (125 ft / 38.1 m) forward and poop deck (105–106 ft / 32 m) aft are at this level. Deck 74 ft 0 in (22.56 m) above keel (§1.3); moulded depth to B 73 ft 3 in. Sources: [BOT Report](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/botRepAccomo.php); [Wikipedia – Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic); Shipbuilder Table II.
* 97 principal staterooms with berths for 198 passengers (BOT). Shipbuilder (Olympic 1911): 30 suite rooms on the Bridge Deck; a B-deck promenade "over 400 ft long and 13 ft minimum width on each side". **On Titanic that B-deck promenade was deleted**: staterooms were extended out to the ship's side, the two Deluxe Parlour Suites got private promenades, the restaurant was enlarged and the Café Parisien added as its annex; Olympic copied these in her 1913 refit ([Wikipedia – Olympic-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-class_ocean_liner); [Chirnside](https://markchirnside.co.uk/faq-did-the-enclosure-of-titanics-a-deck-promenade-increase-her-gross-tonnage/)). Titanic's extra 1,004.75 GRT over Olympic came mostly from this expanded B-deck accommodation ([Chirnside](https://markchirnside.co.uk/faq-did-the-enclosure-of-titanics-a-deck-promenade-increase-her-gross-tonnage/)).

## 9. Known gaps and caveats

* Encyclopedia Titanica, Titanic Wiki (fandom) and the Wayback Machine were not reachable from this environment (HTTP 403/402/connection reset), so frame numbers and some room dimensions published there (Marconi suite, tank rooms, private-promenade width, Café Parisien, Restaurant Reception Room) are not independently verified here.
* The 1911 plates are *Olympic* as built. For Titanic: (1) A-deck forward screen 192–198 ft; (2) B deck: promenade removed, staterooms to the side, two private promenades of 50 ft, enlarged restaurant to port, Café Parisien to starboard, Restaurant Reception Room added; (3) larger officers' deckhouse, Marconi suite on the centreline; (4) Titanic's wheelhouse flat-fronted (forum). Everything else is shared.
* The "Honour and Glory" panel's physical dimensions were not found in any accessible source.
* Positions marked "measured" carry ±10 ft (±3 m). Written dimensions from the 1911 Shipbuilder text and the 1912 Board of Trade report are exact as quoted.
